## Part A. Handbook for assistants (the SHG member and the volunteer)

### A1. What this is, in one minute

You are a pair: one SHG member and one enrolled village volunteer. Together you help an adult who has no phone, or cannot use one, to **report a local problem** (a broken pump, a bad road, a closed school toilet) or to **give feedback on a consultation**. The adult is in front of you, agrees, and the words are theirs.

This is **not** about parties, candidates or elections. It is not a survey. Nothing the person says here is used to measure anyone's opinion.

The record is marked "Reported with assistance" and is shown on its own list, apart from the other reports. It is never counted with them.

### A2. Before you start

- You both sign in on the device, each with your own account. If your partner is not there, you do not record. Never sign in for someone else and never lend your sign-in.
- The person must be an adult (18 or over). If you are not sure, do not record. A child is never an assisted person.
- Do it in person, at an SHG meeting, a village kiosk or a Common Service Centre. Never by phone, and never by knocking on doors to find people to help.
- Women assist women where possible.
- The person must not be pushed. A family head, a leader or anyone else cannot agree on the person's behalf.
- You may not be paid, or praised, or given a target, for each record. If someone offers that, say no and report it (section A9).

### A3. The consent script: read aloud, slowly, then wait

Read it as written. Do not shorten it, do not add to it, and wait for the person to answer in their own words. Then both of you tick the four boxes on the device (adult, explained, willing in their own words, knows deletion is possible). You tick only what really happened.

**English**

> "We are two local volunteers. With your permission we would like to write down a problem in the village for you, so that the right office can see it. 
> We will write only what you tell us, in your words. We will not write your name or phone number, and we will not record your voice.
> This is not about any party, candidate or election. It is not a survey.
> Two of us will see what you say while we type. After that your words appear on a separate list called 'Reported with assistance', without your name.
> You can say no, and nothing happens to you. You can stop at any time.
> You can ask to have it deleted. We will give you a paper with a short code. Keep it. With that code you can ask later what happened, ask for it to be deleted, or tell us if something is wrong.
> Do you agree to us writing this down?"

### A4. If the person says no, or is unsure

Say, and mean:

**English:** "That is fine. Thank you for your time. Nothing will be written, and nothing will happen to you."

**తెలుగు (machine-drafted, needs native-speaker and counsel review):** "సరే, పర్వాలేదు. మీ సమయానికి ధన్యవాదాలు. ఏమీ రాయం, మీకు ఏమీ కాదు."

Then stop. Do not:

- ask again, or explain why it would be good for them;
- ask someone else in the family to agree instead;
- come back another day to try again;
- note down that the person refused, or who they were.

If the person is unsure, let them go home and think. They can come to the next SHG meeting. Do not follow up.

### A5. What you never do

| Never | Why | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **Steer.** Suggest what the problem is, what category it falls under, which office to blame, or what the person "should" ask for | The words must be the person's. Steering is the main way this process goes wrong | Ask open questions: "What would you like to tell us about?" "Where is it?" Then write what they say |
| **Summarise into other words.** Tidy up, shorten, translate meaning, or make it sound better | A record that is not in the person's words is not theirs | Type what they said. Read it back to them, slowly. Change it only when they tell you to |
| **Record party or candidate topics.** Anything about parties, candidates, MLAs, MPs, ministers, campaigns, elections | This process is not for that. The system holds such entries back for review, and a pattern of them will lead to a review of the pair | If the person starts on such a topic, say: "We cannot write that here. Is there a local problem you would like to report?" If they have none, stop |
| **Ask for name, phone number, Aadhaar, age, address, photo or voice.** Or write them in the text | Nothing about the person is stored by design. The system refuses these fields | Do not ask. If the person offers, say "You do not need to tell us that" |
| **Record on behalf of someone who is not there**, or who did not agree themselves | Fabrication | Stop |
| **Record for a child**, or for someone who seems pressed or afraid | Coercion | Stop and, if you are worried, use section A9 |
| **Promise** that the problem will be fixed, or that this helps a party, a leader or a person | You cannot know, and the promise can be a form of pressure | Say what is true: "It will be seen by the right office. We cannot promise what happens next" |
| **Take a gift, money, food or a favour** for recording, or record to reach a number | Paid or quota-driven records are fabricated records | Decline and report it (A9) |
| **Keep a copy** of the person's words, a photo of the screen, or notes in your phone or book | The record exists in one place only | Give the paper receipt; keep nothing else |
| **Share the receipt code** with anyone but the person, or look one up for yourself | The code is the person's only handle | Hand it to the person, once |
| **Use it twice for one person** beyond the limits | Limits exist to stop abuse | Respect the daily limit shown. If the system refuses, do not work around it, for example by using another pair |

### A6. Recording, step by step

1. Introduce yourselves. Ask whether the person has a few minutes. Offer a seat.
2. Read the consent script (A3), wait for the answer.
3. If yes: both of you confirm on the device that the person is an adult and that the four consent steps happened.
4. Ask what they want to report. Let them talk. Ask only "where?" and "since when?" if they have not said.
5. Type their words in Telugu or English, as said. Pick the category the person's words match, only if it is clear; if not, choose the nearest and say nothing more.
6. Read the text back to the person. Correct anything they ask you to change.
7. Submit. The device shows a receipt code **once**. It cannot be shown again.
8. Write the code on the paper slip, or read it out and let the person write it. Give the paper to the person. Check they can read it, or read it to them.
9. Say what comes next (A7). Thank them.

If the system says "held for review", that is normal: a moderator will look at it. Tell the person it may take longer to appear.

### A7. The receipt code, what happens next, and deletion

Say this to the person:

**English:** "This code is yours. It does not have your name on it. With it you can ask what happened to your report, you can ask for it to be deleted, and you can tell us if it was written wrongly or if someone pushed you. Please keep it safe. If you lose it, we cannot find your report for you."

**తెలుగు (machine-drafted, needs native-speaker and counsel review):** "ఈ కోడ్ మీది. దీనిపై మీ పేరు లేదు. దీనితో మీ నివేదికకు ఏమైందో అడగవచ్చు, దాన్ని తొలగించమని కోరవచ్చు, తప్పుగా రాసినా, ఎవరైనా మిమ్మల్ని ఒత్తిడి చేసినా చెప్పవచ్చు. భద్రంగా ఉంచుకోండి. పోగొట్టుకుంటే, మీ నివేదికను మేము వెతికి ఇవ్వలేము."

How it works:

- **The code** has 12 letters and digits, shown like `ABCD-EFGH-JKLM`. The system keeps only a scrambled form of it. Even the operator cannot read your code back from the database.
- **Asking what happened:** the person (or someone they trust, with the paper) enters the code on the public page. It shows whether the report is published, held for review, or deleted, and its status.
- **Deleting:** entering the code and choosing delete erases the words at once. A bare entry stays so that counts and the audit trail still add up; it has no words and no place details beyond the village. Deleting twice does no harm.
- **"This was wrong or pushed on me":** this sends the record to the front of the reviewers' queue, and the pair's other records are checked more closely. The person does not need to give a reason and does not need to give their name.
- If the person cannot use the page, they ask you or any assistant to enter the code for them **in their presence**, or ask the reviewer or the grievance contact (A9). Never enter a code you were not handed that day by the person.

### A8. Code of conduct (each assistant signs a copy of this; the signing is the programme's job)

I will:

1. Help only adults who are present and who agree in their own words.
2. Read the consent script in full, and accept no as no.
3. Write only what the person says, in their words, and read it back.
4. Keep party, candidate and election matters out of this work.
5. Store, ask for, or share nothing about the person: no name, phone number, address, photo, voice, or copy of their words.
6. Never take money, gifts or favours for this work, and never work to a target.
7. Treat everyone the same, whatever their group, party, caste or religion, and accept being paired with people from other groups.
8. Give the receipt to the person and nobody else.
9. Accept a reviewer's visit or call-back to the person, and not try to influence it.
10. Report pressure, misuse and mistakes (including my own) at once, and stop recording while it is looked into.
11. Accept that the programme may suspend me or my pair at any time, and that a suspension is not an accusation.

Breaking the code can end the volunteer's role in the programme. **[VERIFY with counsel: the legal weight of the signed code, and anything that touches paid or contractual status.]**

### A9. Escalation and reporting misuse

| Situation | What to do | Who to tell |
|---|---|---|
| The person seems pressed, afraid, or not the one deciding | Stop. Do not record. Make sure they are safe and not followed | Your coordinator, the same day |
| Someone asks you to record something specific, to meet a number, or for money | Refuse. Do not record | Coordinator, or any reviewer, or the grievance contact |
| You or your partner made a mistake (wrong text, wrong village, recorded without all steps) | Tell the person. If they ask, help them delete it with their receipt | Coordinator |
| A person says a record of theirs is wrong | Help them use the receipt page, in their presence | Coordinator |
| You suspect another pair of faking records | Do not confront them | A reviewer, or the grievance contact, or the operator |
| A person asks you about parties, candidates, or elections | Say you cannot help with that here | Nobody; it is not a report |
| A threat, a safety issue, or a crime | Contact the police and your coordinator as you would for anything else | Coordinator |
| Someone tries to find out who said what | Say it is not recorded | Coordinator |

The grievance contact that does not need a phone, and the names of your coordinator and reviewers, are filled in by the operator before the programme starts: Coordinator ____, Reviewer ____, Grievance contact ____, Operator ____. **[Placeholders. The real grievance officer is a gate item: `docs/RELEASE_GATE.md` row 13.]**

## Part B. Handbook for reviewers

### B1. What a reviewer is

A reviewer is independent of the village: a mandal-level coordinator or moderator, or someone named by the operator. You check, on a sample, that assisted records are real and are the person's own. You do not edit content. You can suspend a pair. You may not re-check a record of your own pair. Reviewers should be rotated.

You need the reviewer role for the village's area. At least two named, trained reviewers should cover each district before go-live.

### B2. How a re-check works

1. A sample of records is queued for re-check. The system queues **every** record of a new pair for its first records (default the first 10), **every** record of a pair that already has a flagged or not-confirmed record, and a random share (default 20%) of the rest. A record the person reported as wrong goes to the front of the queue.
2. You open the re-check queue. Each item shows the category, the village, the text, and a due date (default 7 days from recording). Overdue items are marked.
3. You reach the person. The record holds **no name and no phone**, so you do not call them: you go through the village. Arrange a visit through the SHG or the coordinator, or meet at the SHG meeting. Do **not** ask the pair to bring the person, and do not tell the pair which records are being checked.
4. Meet the person alone, or with someone they choose who is not an assistant. Use the neutral script below.
5. Record the outcome:
   - **confirmed**: the person says they gave it, in their words, willingly.
   - **not_confirmed**: the person says they did not give it, did not agree, or the words were not theirs. The record leaves the public list until a moderator decides.
   - **unreachable**: after a real effort you could not find the person.
6. Note anything relevant in a few words. Never write the person's name or phone in the note.

If the record has been deleted by the person, there is nothing to check and the system will not accept an outcome.

### B3. Neutral script for the re-check

Do not suggest an answer. Do not say the pair did something wrong. Do not name the pair in a way that points at them. Ask the same questions of every person.

**English**

> "Hello. I am (name), from (programme). I am not here for any party or candidate. 
> Some time ago, two volunteers helped to write down a report about (the place or the topic, in general words). I would like to ask whether that is right. You do not have to answer, and nothing will happen to you or to anyone because of what you say.
> 1. Do you remember talking to two volunteers about this?
> 2. Was it your own wish to report this?
> 3. Are the words that were written the ones you meant?
> 4. Did anyone tell you what to say, or ask you for anything for it?
> 5. Would you like it to stay, be changed, or be deleted?
> Thank you."

If the person asks for deletion: tell them how to use the receipt code. If they do not have it, tell your coordinator, who will take it to the operator. **[VERIFY: counsel to say how a deletion request without a code is verified.]**

### B4. How to judge and what to do

| What you find | Outcome | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| The person remembers, agrees it was theirs | confirmed | Nothing more |
| The person does not remember, says they did not give it, or says it was not their wish or words | not_confirmed | Tell the coordinator the same day. Do not name the pair to villagers |
| The person is afraid or says they were pressed | not_confirmed | Treat as misuse (B6); keep the person safe |
| Cannot find the person after a real effort (two tries on different days) | unreachable | Nothing more. A high share of unreachable records is itself a warning, see the runbook |
| The person says yes but seems unsure or influenced | Record what you saw in a short note; choose the outcome that matches what the person actually said | Tell the coordinator if you have doubts |

**The system acts for you.** Not-confirmed records go off the public list. When a pair's not-confirmed share, among checked records, goes above the threshold after enough checks (defaults: above 25% after at least 5 checked), the pair is suspended automatically. Do not tell the pair that they were suspended because of a particular person.

### B5. Reviewer conduct

1. Be independent. If you know the person or the pair well, or have an interest in the topic, hand the item to another reviewer.
2. Ask the questions as written. Do not lead.
3. Never write down the person's name or phone, in the system or in your own notes.
4. Do not discuss re-check outcomes with the pair, or with village leaders.
5. A suspension is a protection step, not a verdict. Say that.
6. Report pressure on yourself at once (B6).

### B6. Escalation and misuse reporting

| What | Who | How fast |
|---|---|---|
| A not-confirmed or fear case | Coordinator, then the operator | Same day |
| Signs of faking (many records, same wording, people who do not exist) | Operator, and suspend the pair | Same day |
| Pressure on you or a threat | Operator and, if needed, the police | Immediately |
| A pair that tries to learn who is being checked | Operator | Same day |
| A party or candidate pushing for or against a pair | Operator | Same day |
| A possible data exposure | The incident steps in `docs/BREACH_RUNBOOK.md`, via the operator | Immediately |

Suspend a pair with a reason of at least 5 characters. The reason and your action go to the audit log.
